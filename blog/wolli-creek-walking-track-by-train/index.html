Wolli Creek, Sydney: gum trees, pelicans and a picnic

Welcome to the Wolli Creek walking track! You'll find pelicans, gum trees and a picnic spot in a gentle half hour of bush from Turrella station on the T8.

Karan Sharma 16 November 2026 3 min read

Hello from one of my favourite quiet corners of Sydney! You're about to meet big gum trees, pelicans and a picnic in the shade, all just off the train line. That's the Wolli Creek walking track.

The T8 does the heavy lifting. Hop off at Turrella, wander through the valley, and hop back on at Bardwell Park. It's 2km one way, about 20 to 30 minutes, so it's a gentle half hour of bush and birdsong. Pack a picnic, a hat, some water and sunscreen. That makes it a lovely afternoon.

How do you get there by train?

Catch the T8 out to Turrella! On a Saturday, the 9.10am from Central gets you there at 9.46am.

From the station, head for Hannam Street, Loftus Street or Reede Street and walk towards the train line. Cross the foot bridge and you're in Turrella Reserve. Ta-da!

That's the middle of the track, and it's a lovely place to start if you've got little ones with you. The Turrella playground has bench seats for a breather.

From the reserve, follow the track towards Girrahween picnic area. Screenshot the map at the station, then enjoy a phone-free half hour with the birds.

What is the walk like?

It's a proper little bush trail, a bit rocky in places, so shoes with grip are perfect. Take your time and enjoy it. You'll feel like a proper bushwalker in no time.

The path changes underfoot as you go. You'll get a bit of rocky trail, then bitumen where it crosses a road and a footpath, then soft grass past Turrella Reserve. A few short, steep hills get the heart going too. Worth it.

Near Girrahween there's one steel step with a handrail. Further along there are eight chunky rock steps. They're great for a breather and a look at the view.

Halfway along you'll pop out at Hartill-Law Avenue. Cross over at a good gap and you're straight back among the gum trees!

Now for the best bit, the birds!

Look for pelicans hunting for fish, and egrets with those long necks. Tawny frogmouths sit in the trees doing their very best to look like a branch, and honestly, they mostly get away with it. Early morning and dusk are the best times to spot them. In spring the wildflowers join the party too.

Then comes Girrahween. It's tucked under big gum trees, with barbecues and two comfy benches, and it's the perfect spot to sit and do absolutely nothing for a while. Bliss. You'll find it at 2 to 10 Sutton Avenue in Earlwood.

Good to know

  • Dogs are welcome on a lead, so bring your four-legged bushwalker.
  • The park is always open, though it can close in poor weather or on a fire danger day.

When is the last train home?

From Girrahween, wander out along Fauna Street to Hartill-Law Avenue. Turn left and stroll down Hartill-Law Avenue to Bardwell Park station, and your train home is waiting.

On a Saturday, trains into the city leave Bardwell Park at 5.05pm, 5.20pm and 5.35pm, then every 15 minutes through the evening. Plenty of time to finish your picnic!

Trains run late on the T8, so linger over that picnic. Saturday night the last one home is 12.20am, and on a Thursday night it's 11.45pm.

Live times will show you exactly what's running, so give them a quick look. Now go and say hi to the pelicans!
