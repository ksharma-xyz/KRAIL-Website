Milk Beach: a Sydney swim and the bus to Nielsen Park

The city skyline over a tiny curve of sand, a swim under giant fig trees, and one bus that drops you right at the park. Here's your perfect Vaucluse day.

Karan Sharma 22 November 2026 4 min read

Oh, you're going to love this one. Picture the whole city skyline sitting behind a tiny curve of sand. That's Milk Beach in Vaucluse, and it's every bit as pretty in real life!

It's the star of a 1.8km harbour walk. At the end, a netted swimming beach waits under huge fig trees, so pack your swimmers and something yummy for later.

And the best bit? One bus does the lot. Hop on the 325, ride to the stop called Nielsen Park, and the walk starts right there in the park.

Your day, in short

Getting there
the 325 bus, to the stop called Nielsen Park
The walk
1.8km one way, 30min to 1hr, Grade 3
The path
a bit of boardwalk, many steps and some bumpy bits, so sneakers are your friend
Swimming
Shark Beach, behind a net in the summer months
Coming home
the last 325 leaves Watsons Bay at 11.04pm, every night

How do you get there from the city?

  1. Jump on the 325 towards Watsons Bay at Town Hall. Catch the 10.13am on a Saturday and you'll be rolling along New South Head Rd through Rose Bay at 10.45am.
  2. Coming by train? Hop off at Edgecliff and pick up the 325 there. It's the easy way in, and that 10.13am trip leaves Edgecliff Station at 10.33am.
  3. Stay on past Rose Bay. The bus turns off New South Head Rd and climbs up Vaucluse Rd, and your stop is called Nielsen Park.
  4. Wander down through the park to Shark Beach. Your harbour day starts here!

Not sure which stop is yours? Find it on the map before you head out, and you'll spot it like a local.

What is the walk like?

It's a 1.8km ramble from Nielsen Park to Bayview Hill Road at Rose Bay, right inside Sydney Harbour National Park. It's a Grade 3 walk, so allow half an hour to an hour, and honestly you'll take longer because of the photos!

It's signposted all the way, so just wander and follow the water. There are plenty of steps, so call it your workout for the day. The path swaps between boardwalk and uneven ground, and sneakers with a bit of grip will keep your feet very happy.

Then comes the moment. Milk Beach sits right below Strickland House, and this is the postcard everyone has seen: the city skyline over a little curve of sand. Pop up and wander the house grounds too. They're open 9am to 5pm every day.

Queens Beach is sand or shallow water depending on the tide, so it's a little surprise every visit. Hermit Point has wooden picnic tables, just right for a snack break. You'll spot Shark Island and the Harbour Bridge out on the water as you go.

Feeling good? Walk all the way to Bayview Hill Road, turn around and stroll back for a 3.6km return, which is exactly how National Parks suggests it. You finish on the sand at Shark Beach, a short wander from the bus.

What's the beach like?

Shark Beach is the reward at the end. It sits beside the kiosk under enormous fig trees, and it has a shark net up in the summer months. The shallows are perfect for a paddle with the little ones. It's a gentle paddle-and-float kind of beach and it isn't patrolled by lifeguards, so grab a friend and make a splash of it. The water quality forecast takes a minute to check before you go in.

Spread your picnic on one of the three sheltered lawns at Nielsen Park, and grab a shady patch under the figs for a post-swim snooze. Hungry? The kiosk has you covered.

Wheelchairs and prams can roll right along the Shark Beach promenade, and a ramp takes you down to the sand. The walking track, with all its steps, is the bumpier part.

What should you pack?

Bring a hat, sunscreen and a water bottle. You can refill it with drinking water at Nielsen Park. The park has flush toilets, including an accessible one, and showers for rinsing off the sand.

Phone reception is patchy in the park, so load anything you need before you set off. Tuck your wrappers in your bag on the way out and the harbour stays this pretty.

It's a people-and-picnic kind of park, so bring the whole gang (the two-legged ones!).

Milk Beach is a weekend favourite, so get there early on a Saturday or Sunday to bag the best spot. The park is open sunrise to sunset, which gives you the whole day to play.

How do you get home?

You finish right by the bus, which is the best kind of ending. The 325 stops at Nielsen Park on Vaucluse Rd and carries you back to the city, sandy toes and all.

Stay as long as you like! The last 325 home leaves Watsons Bay at 11.04pm and stops at Nielsen Park on the way. You're through Rose Bay by 11.14pm and in Town Hall at 11.34pm. It's the same every night of the week, so you can soak up every last golden minute of sunset.
