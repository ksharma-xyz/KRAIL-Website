Auburn Cherry Blossom Festival without a car

Every ticket is booked online, there is no buying one at the gate. Here is the train and free shuttle that gets you there instead.

Karan Sharma 14 August 2026 4 min read

Every ticket to the Sydney Cherry Blossom Festival is booked online this year. Cumberland residents included. There is no buying one at the gate.

The trip, in short

When
22 to 30 August 2026, open 9am to 4:45pm daily
Station
Auburn, on the T2 line, every day
Fare from Central
About $3.76 off peak, $5.38 peak
Last mile
Free shuttle from the platform, every 15 to 20 minutes, 8:30am to 5:30pm
Tickets
Online only, $15.80 adult, $7.90 child 5 to 16, free under 5

That single fact changes how you should plan the trip, and almost nobody who shares this festival mentions it. If you turn up assuming you can pay your way in, you cannot. And if your plan involves driving, you are heading toward limited street parking near a venue that has just told everyone their ticket is already sorted before they arrive, which is exactly the kind of day a car park fills early.

More than 200 cherry trees are blooming in the Japanese Gardens at Auburn Botanic Gardens, 22 to 30 August 2026, and it is genuinely worth the trip. Here is the version where you do not drive.

Getting there

Auburn Station is on the T2 line, which runs there every day, not just weekends. The T1 line also touches Auburn, but only on occasional early morning, late night and weekend services, so T2 is the one to actually plan around.

From Central it is an off-peak fare of about $3.76, or $5.38 at peak. Every weekend of the festival, plus every Friday in the run, is automatically charged the off-peak rate.

From Auburn Station, a free shuttle bus loops between the platform and the gardens every 15 to 20 minutes, running from 8:30am to 5:30pm on festival days. You do not walk it and you do not need to work out a local bus route. Get off the train, get on the shuttle, you are at the gate.

Tickets, and the part that catches people out

Every visitor needs a ticket, and every ticket is booked online in advance, no exceptions for Cumberland residents.

  • Adult, 17 and over: $15.80
  • Children 5 to 16: $7.90
  • Children 4 and under: free
  • Family Pass, 2 adults and 2 children: $44.50
  • Cumberland residents: free, but still requires pre-booking
  • Senior Card holders: $7.90. Companion Card holders: free

The festival is open 9am to 4:45pm daily. The council's own advice, stated plainly on their page, is that weekdays and early mornings are quieter and weekends can sell out.

Do this first Book the ticket before you plan the trip. A sold-out weekend is the one thing a good train plan cannot fix.

If you were going to drive anyway

Street parking near the venue is limited. The council's own overflow options are Tavistock Avenue and Killeen Street, about a 5 minute walk from the gardens, with a small drop-off and pick-up zone also on Killeen Street. There is no dedicated visitor car park at the gate.

Given the shuttle already does the last mile from the station for free, driving buys you a parking search on a weekend the venue itself has flagged as busy, in exchange for skipping a train ride that already ends at the gate.

What is actually there

Beyond the blossoms themselves, which change day to day as they bloom, some trees fuller than others across the nine days and entirely weather dependent, the gardens have a waterfall, a tea house, and a resident colony of wild water dragons in the pond.

Bring water and whatever else you need with you. Bags, hats, prams and umbrellas stay with you the whole visit. The paths stay open in light rain but can get slippery.

Getting back

There is no single last train to plan around here, which is unusual for a KRAIL post. The festival closes at 4:45pm and the shuttle stops at 5:30pm, so you are back at Auburn Station well before the T2 winds down for the night. Trains run back toward the city regularly into the evening. Check the KRAIL app on the day for the next one rather than guessing a platform time in advance.
