Swim in North Bondi's rock pool, then fire up a barbecue

Welcome to North Bondi! You can swim in a rock pool that was carved out of the sandstone, then cook lunch at a free barbecue with a beach view.

Karan Sharma 23 November 2026 3 min read

Welcome to North Bondi! Today you swim in a rock pool that a lifesaver and his team carved out of the sandstone. Then you fire up a free barbecue with Bondi Beach spread out in front of you.

The pool is named after Wally Weekes, and he was quite a character. Lifesaver, boxing champion, hotel owner: Wally did it all!

How do you get to North Bondi?

Take the T4 to Bondi Junction, which is the end of the line. Then head out the Oxford Street side of the station and ride the escalator or the lift down to the buses.

Walk to Stand D and hop on the 379 to North Bondi. Catch the 10.16am on a weekday and you'll be at North Bondi Shops by 10.37am. Slept in? There's another bus about every ten minutes.

Get off at North Bondi Shops on Campbell Parade, which is the last stop. Then follow Ramsgate Avenue to Biddigal Reserve, where the pool is waiting. Off you go!

Tap on and off all day, and an adult's fares stop at $19.30 from Monday to Thursday. From Friday to Sunday, and on public holidays, it's only $9.65.

Fancy the main beach instead? The 333 from Stand A and the 380 from Stand B both go to Bondi Beach.

Who was Wally Weekes?

Wally led the team that carved the pool out of the foreshore sandstone, so children had a safe place to learn to swim.

He was also a founding member of the Bondi Surf and Social Lifesaving Club in 1907, and its first captain. The club's very first clubhouse was a tent on Wally's own land. Yes, a tent!

His land sat among the sandhills at the north end of the beach, a long way from public transport. Lively young men camped there on weekends, right where Biddigal Reserve is today. They surfed, they larked about, and they helped start the club. Lucky for you, there's a bus about every ten minutes now.

Wally was a boxer too, and an amateur lightweight champion by the age of 18. In 1922 he arranged for a slow-motion film to be made of the right way to revive someone. It was meant for Australia, and it ended up being used all over the world.

What's it like to swim in the rock pool?

When the tide comes in, the sea fills Wally Weekes Pool, and that's the moment for a splash. Peek at the tide times before you set off.

Bringing little ones? The North Bondi Children's Pool is right there too, with a playground, barbecues and toilets close by.

What about lunch?

Pack your lunch and make your way to Biddigal Reserve. It has four free barbecue stations that stay open until dusk, and panoramic views of Bondi Beach to go with your sausages.

Spread out and cook up something delicious. Take a long, lazy break while the kids hit the playground, and let the view do the rest.

Want proper laps as well?

Stroll along the beach to the southern end, where Bondi Icebergs sits. The main pool is 50 metres long, and there's a smaller children's pool carved into the cliffs. The club's winter swimming group started in 1929, so you're swimming in history.

Warm up in the sauna afterwards, because it's included in the price of your pool ticket. Then head upstairs to The Bergs Bistro, where everyone is welcome to eat and drink on the sunny balcony. Check the opening times before you go.

How do you get home?

Catch the 379 back from North Bondi Shops to Bondi Junction. The last bus leaves at 12.56am and gets in at 1.09am.

Stay out as late as you like! Trains from Bondi Junction run past midnight every night, and later still on Fridays and Saturdays. Catch the 12.16am bus, which reaches the station at 12.29am, and you'll make the last train on any night of the week.

At Bondi Junction, ride the lift straight up to the trains. Platforms 1 and 2 both take you towards the city.

Pop open the live times before a big night out.

Want the whole coast while you're here? The walk from Bondi to Coogee links up the beaches.

Now go and find the pool that was cut from the rock!
