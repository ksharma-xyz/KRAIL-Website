There is a bush track in the inner south-west with a train station at each end. Get off the T8 at
Turrella, walk the valley, and get back on at Bardwell Park. There is no car parked at the wrong end
of it.

It is 2km. This is not a big day out. It is half an hour of trees and birds in the middle of a city.
That is a strange and good thing to have sitting on your own line.

::: facts The walk, in short
Track: **2km** one way, **20 to 30 minutes**, grade 3
Getting there: the **T8** to Turrella
Coming home: the **T8** from Bardwell Park
Signage: limited, so know the shape of the walk first
Bring: a hat, water and sunscreen
Dogs: on a lead
:::

## How do you get there without a car?

Take a T8 train to Turrella. On a Saturday the 9.10am from Central is at Turrella at 9.46am.

Leave the station and head for Hannam Street, Loftus Street or Reede Street. Walk towards the train
line. Cross the foot bridge into Turrella Reserve.

That is the middle of the track. There are bench seats at Turrella playground midway, so this is a
good end to start from with small children.

From the reserve, follow the track towards Girrahween picnic area. Signage is limited, so it is
worth having the shape of it in your head before you lose reception.

## The walk itself

The path is half a metre to a metre wide and mostly hard packed ground. In parts it is very rocky
and uneven. There are pinch points where rocks narrow it right in.

There are short steep hills. None of it needs any experience.

The surface keeps changing. It turns to bitumen where it crosses a road and a footpath. It turns to
grass as it goes past Turrella Reserve.

Two sets of steps are worth knowing about. There are 8 rock steps without handrails towards the
Jackson Place end. There is 1 steel step with a handrail near Girrahween.

The one real interruption is Hartill-Law Avenue. The track crosses it, it is a main road, and it can
be hard to get across.

What you came for is the birds. Look for pelicans hunting for fish and egrets with those long necks.
Tawny frogmouths sit in the trees doing their best to look like a branch. Early morning and dusk are
when they are about. In spring there are wildflowers along the track.

Girrahween sits under big eucalypts and has barbecues. It is a good place to stop and not go
anywhere for a while.

## The practical stuff

- **Picnic** at Girrahween, at 2 to 10 Sutton Avenue in Earlwood. It has barbecues.
- **Seats**: two with backrests near Girrahween, two more at the Turrella playground midway.
- **Phone reception** is limited in the park. Load what you need before you go in.
- **Signage** is limited too, which is the reason to look at the map first.
- **Steps**: 8 rock steps without handrails near the Jackson Place end.
- **Hartill-Law Avenue** cuts across the track and can be hard to cross.
- **Dogs** are fine on a lead.
- **Wheels**: the access rating is hard, so a pram or a wheelchair will struggle on the rocky parts.
- **Bring** a hat, water and sunscreen.
- **The park** is always open, but it can close in poor weather or on a fire danger day.

## Getting back, and the one time to know

From Girrahween, Fauna Street takes you out to Hartill-Law Avenue. Bardwell Park station is south
along it.

City bound trains leave Bardwell Park every 15 minutes through the afternoon and into the evening.
On a Saturday that is 5.05pm. Then 5.20pm. Then 5.35pm, and on it goes. You do not have to plan
around it.

The one to know is the last one. On a Saturday night the last train into the city goes at 12.20am.
Sunday to Thursday nights it is much earlier, just before midnight.

Here is the part that catches people. Trains the other way, towards Macarthur, keep running past
12.25am on those nights. So a station with trains still coming through it is not proof that yours is
one of them.

Check the [live times](/blog/live-sydney-train-bus-ferry-times/) before you walk back up Fauna
Street. A timetable is the plan. The countdown is what is actually happening.
