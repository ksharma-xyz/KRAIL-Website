Milk Beach is the one with the city skyline standing right behind it. Shark Beach is the one with the
net. Both sit on a 1.8km walk along the harbour at Vaucluse, and one bus stops at the gate.

The parking is Greycliffe Avenue, and it is signed. On a warm Sunday that is the whole problem. Milk
Beach fills up early, and you end up circling a residential street for a gap that is not there.

Take the 325 instead. There is a stop called Nielsen Park. Bring your swimmers and something to eat.

::: facts The trip, in short
Getting there: the **325** bus, to the stop called **Nielsen Park**
The walk: **1.8km** one way, **30min to 1hr**, Grade 3
Underfoot: boardwalk, uneven ground and **many steps**
Swimming: **Shark Beach**, behind a net in the summer months
Coming home: the last **325** leaves Watsons Bay at **11.04pm**, every night
:::

## How do you get there without a car?

1. Catch the 325 towards Watsons Bay. On a Saturday the 10.13am from Town Hall is on New South Head
   Rd at Rose Bay at 10.45am.
2. Or take a train to Edgecliff and pick the bus up there. Between 11am and midday on a Saturday,
   four of these buses leave Edgecliff and only two leave Town Hall.
3. Stay on past Rose Bay. The bus turns off New South Head Rd and up Vaucluse Rd, and your stop is
   called Nielsen Park.
4. Walk down through the park to Shark Beach. The track starts here.

If you are not sure which stop is yours, [find it on the map](/blog/find-sydney-stop-on-map/) before
you go.

## The walk itself

The track runs 1.8km one way, from Nielsen Park to Bayview Hill Road at Rose Bay. National Parks
calls it Grade 3 and suggests 30 minutes to an hour. It is clearly signposted and needs no experience.

What the grade does not tell you is the steps. There are many of them, and the path keeps swapping
between boardwalk and uneven ground. Wear shoes with some grip.

Milk Beach sits directly below Strickland House. That is the view everyone has seen, the city
skyline over a small curve of sand. The house grounds are open 9am to 5pm daily.

Queens Beach is either sand or shallow water, depending on the tide. Hermit Point has wooden picnic
tables. Shark Beach, back at Nielsen Park, has the net and the kiosk under the fig trees.

The far end of the track is Bayview Hill Road at Rose Bay. Turn around there and the day is 3.6km,
which is how National Parks offers it. You finish back at the beach and the bus stop.

## The practical stuff

- **Toilets** at Nielsen Park, flush, including an accessible one.
- **Water** from the drinking fountain at Nielsen Park.
- **Food** at the kiosk in the park, under enormous fig trees.
- **Swimming** at Shark Beach, behind a net in the summer months.
- **Lifeguards**: none. These beaches are not patrolled and the rips can be strong.
- **Picnic** tables at Hermit Point, and three sheltered areas on the lawns at Nielsen Park.
- **Shade** on the lawns at Nielsen Park, from the fig trees.
- **Shoes**: something with grip. Many steps, and some uneven ground.
- **Phone reception** is limited in the park. Load what you need before you start.
- **Wheelchairs and prams**: the Shark Beach promenade is easy, with a ramp down to the sand. The
  track itself is not.
- **Dogs** are not allowed here at any time.
- **Rubbish**: there are no bins along the track, so carry yours out.
- **Crowds**: Milk Beach fills up early on a weekend. Go in the morning.
- **Closing time**: the park is open sunrise to sunset.

## Getting back

The worry with a one way walk is finishing somewhere with no way out. Here it is not much of a
worry.

The 325 runs back past Nielsen Park to the city. Check which side of Vaucluse Rd your bus goes from
before you settle in.

The last one towards the city leaves Watsons Bay at 11.04pm and calls at Nielsen Park on the way. It
is at Rose Bay by 11.14pm and Town Hall at 11.34pm.

That is the same time every night of the week, Saturday and Sunday included. The park closes at
sunset, so you are out of there long before the bus stops running.
