A clear pool in the middle of the bush, with Gymea lilies leaning over the water. On a hot day you
get in.

The track to it is rough and the signs are few. Both ends of your day are a small station a long
way south of the city. That part is on us.

Take your swimmers and your lunch. There are picnic tables at the pool, so you may as well eat
there.

::: facts The walk, in short
Getting there: the **T4** to Waterfall, about an hour from Central
The track: **5.5km** one way, **3 to 4 hours**, **Grade 4**
Coming home: the **T4** from Heathcote
Swim: Kingfisher Pool, a freshwater stream with picnic tables
Toilets: at Waterfall station. There are none in the park
:::

## How do you get there without a car?

1. Take the T4 to Waterfall. On a weekend the 8.37am from Central is at Waterfall at 9.35am.
2. Use the toilet at the station before you go anywhere. There are none in the park.
3. Fill every bottle you have. There is no drinking water in the park either.
4. Turn right onto Kooraban Street, then right again onto Warabin Street.
5. Walk to the end of Warabin Street. The trail carries on, and 50m along it the Bullawarring
   walking track is signposted.

Weekend trains to Waterfall come about every half hour through the middle of the day. So if you miss
one you have not lost the day.

## The track itself

It is 5.5km one way, and three to four hours is the time to allow.

Grade 4. That means limited signage and a rough path with many obstacles. There are short steep hills
and occasional steps. It is a track for experienced bushwalkers.

Walking out at the Heathcote end adds to that 5.5km. So treat any total you have seen for this day
as somebody's estimate, and give yourself longer.

Bring a topographic map and compass, or a GPS. With signage this limited you will want one.

Tell someone where you are going before you lose reception.

## The pool in the middle

Kingfisher Pool picnic area sits part way along the track, north-west of Waterfall station.

It is a freshwater stream, fringed by Gymea lilies, tea trees and waxflowers. In summer a dip here is
the point of the whole walk.

There are picnic tables. There is no tap and no toilet.

## How do you get out at Heathcote?

From the pool you have three ways on.

1. Turn back on the Mooray walking track for a loop and return to Waterfall.
2. Carry on along Pipeline trail at Battery Causeway until you reach the natural pools.
3. Keep walking north along Pipeline trail to Goburra track. Join Oliver Street and head back via
   Heathcote station.

Option three is the one that makes this a station to station day. Heathcote is one stop closer to
the city than Waterfall, so your train home is a little shorter than your train out.

## The practical stuff

- **Water**: carry all of it, for the whole day. There is none in the park.
- **Toilets**: none in the park. The closest are at Waterfall station.
- **Picnic tables** at Kingfisher Pool. Bring the lunch yourself.
- **Heat**: summer here averages 16 to 27 degrees. The highest ever recorded is 42.
- **Signs** are limited. This is the map and compass walk, not the follow the arrows walk.
- **Dogs** are not allowed. Assistance animals are.
- **Park hours**: sunrise to sunset. It can close for poor weather or fire danger, so check before
  you leave home.
- **Swimming**: creeks and pools are unpredictable. Read the water before you jump.

## The last train home

The park shuts at sunset, so you should be out long before any of this matters. Have the numbers
anyway.

Trains from Heathcote towards the city come about every 30 minutes.

- **Monday to Thursday**, the last train that reaches the city leaves Heathcote at 11.29pm. It is at
  Central at 12.19am.
- **Friday** gets one more, at 11.59pm, into Central at 12.49am.
- **Saturday and Sunday** nights, the last one leaves at 12.36am and reaches Central at 1.19am.

Two late trains are not a way home. The 12.29am on a weekday, and the 11.36pm on a Sunday, both
finish at Hurstville and stop there.

So check the live times at the top of the last climb, while you still have a bar of signal.

Then you walk out knowing exactly what you are walking to.
