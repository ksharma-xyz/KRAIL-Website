Follow a creek over timber boardwalks and through hanging swamps until it tips over a cliff, then
stand at the top of the waterfall with the whole Jamison Valley in front of you.

You will be in the Blue Mountains, you will not have driven, and you can be back in the city for
dinner. If you have just moved to Sydney and everyone keeps telling you the mountains need a car,
this is the walk that proves otherwise. It is called Darwins Walk, and you may also see it written
as the Charles Darwin Walk.

::: facts The day, in short
Getting there: a **Blue Mountains line** train to Wentworth Falls, and the walk starts across the road
Distance: **2.4km one way**, 45 minutes to an hour and a quarter, Grade 3
At the end: the top of Wentworth Falls, with the Jamison Valley in front of you
Best day to go: Friday to Sunday and public holidays, when the daily cap is **$9.65** for the whole day
Reopened: September 2025, after four years shut
:::

## It has only just come back

Darwins Walk spent four years closed. Successive floods took out the footbridges and tore up the
track, and the repair was big enough that the council did it in two halves: the upper section from
Wilson Park reopened in March 2025, and the harder lower section, which crosses a nationally listed
endangered peat swamp on raised walkway, opened that September.

So anything you read about this walk from before 2025 was written about a track nobody could use.
That is the good news. The rest of this page is what it actually takes to get there.

## Your day, start to finish

Catch a Blue Mountains Line train from Central to Wentworth Falls. It takes about an hour and fifty
minutes, so treat this as a day rather than a morning.

Get your coffee and breakfast in the village when you arrive, because there are cafes right there
and there is nothing at all on the track. Use the toilets in the village while you are at it. Then
cross the Great Western Highway to Wilson Park picnic area, pick up the track heading south, and
follow Jamison Creek.

Allow 45 minutes to an hour and a quarter to reach the national park boundary, which is 2.4
kilometres of walking. From there it is 400 metres to Wentworth Falls by way of Weeping Rock, or
about 15 minutes to the Wentworth Falls picnic area.

Walk back the same way when you are done, or take Falls Road from the picnic area straight back
into town. Both put you at the station.

## Getting on the right train

Look for the Blue Mountains Line on the boards at Central. Every train heading for Katoomba, Mount
Victoria or Lithgow stops at Wentworth Falls, and you stay on the same train the whole way. Coming
home you want platform 1, the Sydney side.

Some real times, so you can see the shape of it:

- **On a weekday**, the 7:25am from Central gets you to Wentworth Falls at 9:16am. The 8:25am gets
  you there at 10:15am.
- **On a weekend or public holiday**, trains leave Central about every half hour through the
  morning. The 8:24am arrives at 10:15am.

Check for trackwork before you leave. Replacement buses run slower than trains on this line, and
finding out at Central costs you the morning.

## The walk itself

Grade 3, which the council translates as rough surfaces and many steps, and then adds that it suits
most ages and fitness levels. That is a fair description. It is not a stroll and it is not a
scramble either.

Watch for the yellow-tailed black cockatoos. National Parks uses them as the wayfinding markers
along here, which is a nice touch, and the real ones are usually somewhere in the banksias making a
racket. Honeyeaters and shrub wrens work the lower scrub.

Take your time at the rock pools and cascades along Jamison Creek. Swimming is not advised, and the
council is blunt about why: creek levels rise fast during and after rain with very little warning.
Never walk into flood water here.

Bring drinking water, a hat, sunscreen and shoes with a bit of grip. There is nothing to buy once
you leave the village. Watch where you put your feet on warm days as well, because snakes like this
track too.

## Bringing a dog

Dogs are allowed on lead along Darwins Walk, because Blue Mountains City Council manages it, not the
national park. Almost every other walk this good in the mountains is closed to them.

The exception is the last 40 metres before Weeping Rock, where the track crosses into Blue Mountains
National Park. That boundary is signposted and dogs cannot go past it, so with a dog you turn around
there and walk back the way you came. You still get the creek, the swamps and the boardwalks, just
not the waterfall.

One warning worth having in advance. Some of the bridges are steel serrated and some of the
walkways are raised well off the ground, and plenty of dogs hate the feel of that underfoot or the
sight of the drop through it.

## What is open, and what is not

Darwins Walk itself has no alerts on it. Several of its famous neighbours do, and it is worth
knowing before you build a bigger day around it: part of National Pass is shut after a landslide,
Wentworth Pass and Slack Stairs are closed for boardwalk repairs, and Valley of the Waters is closed
below Silvia Falls after rockfall.

None of that touches the creek walk or the lookouts above the falls. Stand at Fletchers lookout and
you watch Wentworth Falls drop 100 metres into the Jamison Valley, and that view is open.

If you want more, this is the first leg of the Grand Cliff Top Walk, which runs 19 kilometres over
two days from here to Katoomba. Both ends of it are train stations.

## What it costs

The walk is free. You book nothing and you pay nobody at the gate.

Wentworth Falls is far enough out to sit in the top fare distance band, and that is
where the day gets interesting. Go on a Friday, Saturday, Sunday or a public holiday and every fare
is discounted and the daily cap is $9.65, so the return trip and everything you tap to reach Central
comes to $9.65 for the whole day. A single trip out on those days is $7.46 on its own, so the cap
does almost all of the work.

Monday to Thursday you will pay more. A trip in that top band costs $10.66 at peak times and $7.46
outside them, and the cap for those days sits at $19.30.

Tap on and tap off with the same card or phone you already tap with everywhere
else in the city.

## The last train home

This is the part to plan around, because the trains thin out to one an hour in the evening and the
ride back is nearly two hours.

**Monday to Friday**, the last train leaves Wentworth Falls at 10:25pm and gets into Central at
12:17am. There is another at 9:25pm if you would rather have an hour in hand.

**On a Saturday, Sunday or public holiday**, the last one leaves at 11:18pm and gets in at 1:12am.
Look at the one before it though, because it goes at 9:48pm. There is nothing between those two, so
a train you miss at 9:48pm on a Sunday night leaves you sitting on the platform for an hour and a
half.

None of this is hidden. It is printed in the timetable. It is simply not the sort of thing anyone
thinks to read before a two hour walk, and it is the only part of this day that can really go wrong.
