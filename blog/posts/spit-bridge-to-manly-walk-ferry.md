Walk 10km of harbour shoreline and finish on the Manly Corso. Catch a bus out to Spit Bridge in
the morning, then ride the ferry home from Manly. Drive it instead and your car spends the day at
the Spit while you stand in Manly.

Bring your swimmers. Clontarf, Reef Beach and Forty Baskets are all on the way.

**It is a one way walk.** That is the whole case for leaving the car at home. The fear that puts
people in a car is finishing 10km from where they started with no way back. On this one the way
back is the best part of the day.

::: facts The walk, in short
Getting there: the **173X** from Wynyard, about 17 minutes to the Spit
The walk: **10km** one way, **3hr 30min to 4hr 30min**, Grade 3
Underfoot: short steep hills and **many steps**
Coming home: the **F1** ferry from Manly Wharf 1
Toilets: Clontarf, Reef Beach, Forty Baskets and North Harbour Reserve
Dogs: not allowed, it runs through a national park
:::

## How do you get there without a car?

Catch the 173X from Wynyard Station, Carrington St, Stand A. On a Saturday morning it goes every 20
minutes. The 9am is at Spit West Reserve at 9.17am. Get off there.

Coming from the north instead, take the 144 from Chatswood. It leaves from Victoria Ave, Stand F,
and stops at the same place. The 9.04am is at Spit West Reserve at 9.37am, and on a Saturday morning they run
every seven or eight minutes.

If you are not sure which stand is yours, you can
[find it by the route number](/blog/find-sydney-bus-stop-by-number/).

The walk starts on the north side of the bridge. From the bus stop, follow Spit Rd across.

## What is the walk actually like?

It is 10km one way and takes 3hr 30min to 4hr 30min. Grade 3, so some bushwalking experience
helps. The track is sign posted the whole way.

What catches people out is the shape of it. This is a formed track with some obstacles, short steep
hills and many steps. It is not a flat harbour promenade.

At high tide the track detours in places. Check the tide before you go if you would rather walk the
shoreline than the detour.

First comes Fisher Bay. Then Sandy Bay and Clontarf Beach. Castle Rock is where you enter Sydney
Harbour National Park. After that comes Dobroyd Head, the Grotto Point Aboriginal rock
engravings and the Grotto Point Lighthouse.

Stop at Arabanoo Lookout. It looks out over North Head, South Head and the open ocean, and people
watch for whales from it. The lookout is named after Arabanoo, the first Aboriginal man to live
among European settlers.

Reef Beach and Forty Baskets come near the end. Then the shoreline turns into Manly, and the Corso
is waiting with an ice cream.

## The practical stuff

- **Toilets** at Clontarf, Reef Beach, Forty Baskets Beach and North Harbour Reserve.
- **Swimming and picnics** at Clontarf Reserve, Reef Beach or Forty Baskets Reserve.
- **Rips** are a real thing here. None of these beaches is patrolled.
- **Dogs** are not allowed. Certified assistance animals are.
- **Phone reception** is limited. Download what you need before you start.
- **Sign posted** the whole way, so there is nothing to navigate.
- **Sunrise to sunset** is when the national park is open. It can close for weather or fire danger.
- **Times here are weekend times.** Check your own trip before you go on a weekday.

## When is the last ferry home from Manly?

Not for a long time, which is the point.

The F1 leaves Manly Wharf 1 every 15 minutes through a Saturday afternoon. The 4pm is at Circular
Quay at 4.22pm. From 8pm it goes every half hour instead.

The last one on a Saturday night leaves Manly at 12.50am and is at Circular Quay at 1.12am. On a
Sunday night the last one goes at 11.50pm and gets in at 12.12am.

The park closes at sunset, so you are off the track hours before any of that.
