Swim at MacCallum Pool, cut into the rock at Cremorne Point, walk the headland with water on both
sides, then have lunch at Mosman Bay. You can do it all in an afternoon, and you never need a car.

If you have just moved to Sydney, make this your first one. A ferry drops you at one end of the walk
and collects you from the other, so there is almost nothing to plan. Almost. One thing catches people
out, and it is worth reading to the bottom for.

::: facts The afternoon, in short
Getting there: the **F6** ferry from Circular Quay, 10 minutes to Cremorne Point
The walk: around the headland, and nobody publishes a time for it, so give it an afternoon
Coming home: the F6 again, from the wharf at Mosman Bay
The catch: the last Sunday boat goes at **9:22pm**, three hours earlier than a Saturday
Weekends: hourly both days, so a boat you miss costs a full hour
:::

## How do you do it without a car?

Catch the F6 ferry from Circular Quay to Cremorne Point. Step off the wharf and you are already
standing at the start of the track, so keep walking. Follow the path around the headland until you
come out at Mosman Bay, and the boat home leaves from the wharf waiting there.

No council publishes a time for this one, so do not plan it to the minute. Give it an afternoon,
because you are going to want to stop.

## Getting on the right boat

Look for the **F6** on the departure boards at Circular Quay. It takes 10 minutes to reach Cremorne
Point, and you stay on the same boat the whole way.

Do not memorise a wharf number. The published timetable warns that the departure wharf at Circular
Quay can change at short notice, so read the indicator boards each time. Sydney locals do this too.

How often it runs depends on the day:

- **Monday to Friday**, about every half hour through the day, then hourly after 7pm.
- **Saturday and Sunday**, hourly all day.
- The first Sunday boat out of Circular Quay is **9am**. If you were hoping for a 7am or 8am start,
  those are Saturday only.

## What to do along the way

**Swim at MacCallum Pool.** It is a 33 metre pool on the western side of Cremorne Point, sitting
right on the harbour, and it is the reason to bring your swimmers. One catch: it is drained and
cleaned most weeks, and because the cleaning has to follow the tide the closure times move around.
North Sydney Council publishes the dates, so check them the morning you go.

**Walk through the Lex and Ruby Graham Gardens.** Two people started this in 1957 with a single
elephant's ear plant and kept going for decades. The National Trust protects it now. There is no
gate, no entry fee and no formal entrance, so watch for the planted slope above the water or you will
walk through it without realising what you are in.

**Stop at Robertsons Point Lighthouse.** Take the view from the lookout above it and stay there. The
way down to the water is old steps and a ladder, and the view from the top is the one people come
for anyway.

**Sit down.** There are picnic lawns along the reserve looking straight across at the city. There is
a cafe at each end of the walk, and toilets within 100 metres of Cremorne Point wharf, which is worth
knowing before you set off rather than after.

It is a foreshore path rather than a bush track, which is the reason it works with kids.

## How to get home, and the Sunday catch

The F6 turns around at Mosman Bay and goes back to Circular Quay the way it came. Finish the walk,
sit down at the wharf, and get on the boat.

**Monday to Saturday**, the last one leaves Mosman Bay at 12:22am and gets into Circular Quay at
12:40am. Lunch can turn into the whole afternoon and it will not cost you anything.

**On a Sunday, the last one leaves at 9:22pm** and gets in at 9:40pm. The 10:22pm, 11:22pm and
12:22am services run on Saturdays only, so the same walk gives you three fewer hours. Public holidays
follow the Sunday pattern.

Two more things worth knowing. On both weekend days the F6 is hourly, so a boat you miss costs you a
full hour rather than ten minutes. And none of this is hidden: it is printed in the timetable, in a
row of day restrictions above the times. It is just not the sort of thing anyone thinks to read
before an afternoon walk, and it is the only part of this trip that can go wrong.

## What it costs

It will be a normal fare, just like any other ferry. Tap on when you get on the boat and tap off
when you step onto the wharf, using the same contactless card or phone you already
tap with everywhere else in the city.

Everything else about the day is free. You do not book anything, and the walk, the gardens and the
pool do not cost you a cent.
