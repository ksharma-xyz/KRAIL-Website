Up the Irons, you're off to see Iron Maiden at Allianz Stadium on Sunday 15 November! It's going to
be loud, sweaty and absolutely brilliant.

Gates open at 5.30pm and Maiden are on at 6.10pm, so come early, grab a spot and soak it all in.
They go right through to 10.50pm. That's a lot of Eddie for your money!

Now here's the best bit, and you'll love it before you even leave home. Your show ticket is your
ride! It covers public transport there and back, light rail included, right up until 4am. And the
light rail drops you practically at the stadium door.

::: callout Keep your ticket handy
Once you're through the gate, pop your show ticket somewhere easy to reach. It's your ride home too,
and you just show it to the staff when you hop on.
:::

## How do you get to Moore Park?

Make your way to Central by train or metro. Then pop out to the light rail stop on Chalmers Street
and jump on an L2 or an L3 heading south.

Hop off at Moore Park. The stop sits on Anzac Parade, just south of the stadium, so you'll hear the
crowd before you see it.

On Sunday afternoon a light rail comes along every five minutes, and after 7pm it's one every eight.
There are extra services before and after the show too, so you'll be on your way in no time.

Coming by ferry? Lucky you, that's the scenic way in! Hop off at Circular Quay, where the L2 and the
L3 both start, and ride all the way to Moore Park without changing once.

## Coming from the east?

Coming from Bondi Junction? Hop on a 333 or 352 to Oxford Street and follow the black T-shirts,
because everyone's heading the same way. It's a short, happy walk in from there.

## Fancy a walk instead?

Central to Moore Park is about 25 minutes on foot, which is just enough time to warm up your singing
voice. Head along Devonshire Street, then cross the Albert 'Tibby' Cotter Walkway, a bridge that
carries you right over the traffic on Anzac Parade.

Walking in is half the fun: you stroll along with the whole crowd, singing as you go, and arrive
nicely warmed up.

## How do you get home after the encore?

The L2 and the L3 keep running until 1am, and extra services kick in as soon as the show ends. The
metro runs until 12.30am that night, and your ticket gets you home any time until 4am on Monday
morning.

Everyone heads for the light rail at once, so give yourself a little extra time. Hum your favourite
chorus, soak up the buzz, and let the crowd thin out a bit before you join the line.

Peek at the [live times](/blog/live-sydney-train-bus-ferry-times/) while you wait, so you can see
exactly what's rolling in next.

## Coming from somewhere else? I've got you

Coming from the Central Coast? There's trackwork up near Gosford that afternoon and evening. Open KRAIL
and it'll show you the best way in.

Flying in? Lovely! Your ticket even covers the train from the airport, so you can come straight off
the plane and into the show. (Just the airport station fee on top.)

Fancy a cab home? The taxi rank is on Errol Flynn Boulevard, near the Hordern Pavilion. Grabbing a
rideshare? Wander a few minutes from the stadium, let the singing wind down, and meet your driver
somewhere calm.

Driving in? Leave the car at a station you like and ride in with the crowd, which is a fun start to
the night. Park & Ride is free for up to 18 hours. You can
[see how many spaces are left](/blog/sydney-park-and-ride-live-spaces/) before you go. On the way
out, just show your event ticket at the intercom by the exit gate.

The light rail drops you right by the stadium, so save your energy for the encore!

Come early, soak up the pre-show buzz, and have the best night. Scream for me, Sydney!
