Oh, you're going to love this one! The Spit to Manly walk gives you bush tracks, sandy beaches and
big harbour views, then a ferry waiting at Manly takes you home.

Pack your swimmers! Clontarf, Reef Beach and Forty Baskets are all on the way, and each one is very
good at talking you into a quick dip.

It's a 10km walk that takes about four hours once you've had a swim or two. There are lots of steps and
some short steep bits along the way, and the views make every one worth it.

Start by catching the ferry out to Manly. Then hop on the 144 to the Spit and walk yourself all the
way back to the wharf. Your legs will thank you at the end.

It's a one-way walk, which is the best kind: you never walk back the way you came. The ferry brings
you home.

## How do you get to the start?

You can catch the ferry from Circular Quay out to Manly. On a weekend the 9.45am gets in at 10.07am,
and a ride across the harbour is a lovely way to wake up.

Before you leave the wharf, pop into the Manly Visitor Information Centre right next to it and grab
a printed map. They're friendly there!

Then wander around to Belgrave Street, beside the wharf, and hop on the 144 towards Chatswood. It
leaves at 10.20am on both Saturday and Sunday.

Hop off at Spit East Reserve, the last stop before the bridge. If you'd like to find that stop ahead
of time, you can [look it up by the route number](/blog/find-sydney-bus-stop-by-number/).

Follow the signs from the Spit Bridge and they'll walk you all the way to Manly.

## What's the walk like?

Give yourself three and a half to four and a half hours, because you'll want to stop a lot.

It's a real bush track, so wear shoes with good grip and you'll feel like a proper explorer out
there.

The first stretch follows the shore past Fisher Bay, Sandy Bay and Clontarf Beach, which is a lovely
way to start. Then the track enters Sydney Harbour National Park at Castle Rock, and the hills come
out to say hello.

After that you reach Dobroyd Head and the Aboriginal rock engravings at Grotto Point. Take your time
there. Then comes Arabanoo lookout, the last big view before Manly. Stop for it, even if your legs
have opinions.

Fancy a shorter day? You can join or leave the track at Clontarf Reserve, Cutler Road Lookout, Tania
Park or Wellings Reserve, as well as at either end.

## What else is handy to know?

- **Toilets** are at Clontarf, Reef Beach, Forty Baskets Beach and North Harbour Reserve.
- **Swimming** is at Clontarf, Forty Baskets and Reef Beach. They're quiet beaches with no lifeguards, so a splash close to the sand is perfect.
- **Bringing a dog?** The council has a separate 2km dog route just outside the national park.
- **Maps** are at the Manly Visitor Information Centre, right next to Manly Wharf.
- **Castle Rock** is getting an upgrade, so there's a detour there. Peek at the alerts before you set off and you'll know what's open.

Phone reception comes and goes out there, so download your map before you set off. Then you can just
enjoy the peace.

Start early and the day is all yours. The park is open from sunrise to sunset, and the best views
come near the end, so you'll catch them in lovely daylight.

## When is the last ferry home?

You finish at Manly Wharf, and your ferry is right there. Nice work!

The ferries run lovely and late, so you can finish the walk at your own pace. From Monday to
Thursday, the last ferry to Circular Quay leaves at 12.20am, and on a Friday or Saturday night it's
12.50am. On a Sunday night it's 12.20am again, just after midnight.

Wander down to the wharf with a little time to spare, because the gates close two minutes before each
ferry. That way you get the pick of the seats.

Peek at the [live times](/blog/live-sydney-train-bus-ferry-times/) before you start the last stretch.
A timetable shows what's planned, and live times show what's actually running.

Then settle in on the ferry and watch the city lights come closer across the water. You earned them.
