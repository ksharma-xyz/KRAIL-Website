The walk from the Spit Bridge to Manly is 10km, and it only goes one way. Drive it and your car
spends the day at the wrong end. Catch the ferry to Manly instead, take the 144 back to the Spit,
and walk home to the wharf.

Bring your swimmers. Clontarf, Reef Beach and Forty Baskets are all on the way, and you will stop at
more of them than you meant to.

::: facts The walk, in short
Getting there: the ferry to Manly, then route **144** to the Spit
The walk: **10km** one way, **3hrs 30min to 4hrs 30min**
Grade: **3**, many steps and some short steep hills
Coming home: the ferry from Manly Wharf, the last one after midnight
Dogs: not in the national park
:::

Doing it in this direction is the whole trick. You finish at a wharf instead of at a bus stop on
Spit Road.

## How do you get to the start without a car?

Take the ferry from Circular Quay to Manly. On a weekend the 9.50am is at Manly at 10.12am.

Walk around to Belgrave Street, beside the wharf, and catch the 144 towards Chatswood. The 10.20am
goes on both weekend days.

Get off at Spit East Reserve. That is the last stop before the bridge. If you are not sure which
stop is yours, you can [find it by the route number](/blog/find-sydney-bus-stop-by-number/).

The Spit Bridge is one of the six marked ways on to the walkway, and the track is signposted from
there to Manly. Pick up a printed map at the Manly Visitor Information Centre next to the wharf
before you get on the bus.

## What is the walk actually like?

It is 10km one way and takes three and a half to four and a half hours.

The grade is 3. That means a formed track with some obstacles, many steps, and short steep hills.
Wear something with grip.

You follow the shoreline first, past Fisher Bay, Sandy Bay and Clontarf Beach. Then the track enters
Sydney Harbour National Park at Castle Rock.

After that comes Dobroyd Head and the Aboriginal rock engravings at Grotto Point. The view from
Arabanoo lookout is the last one before Manly.

There are six ways on and off the track. The Spit Bridge and Manly Wharf are the two ends. In
between you can join or leave at Clontarf Reserve, Cutler Road Lookout, Tania Park or Wellings
Reserve.

## The practical stuff

- **Toilets** at Clontarf, Reef Beach, Forty Baskets Beach and North Harbour Reserve.
- **Swimming** at Clontarf, Forty Baskets and Reef Beach. None of these beaches are patrolled.
- **Steps**, plenty of them, plus short steep hills after a flat opening stretch.
- **Phone reception** is limited along the track.
- **Park hours** are sunrise to sunset.
- **Dogs** are not allowed in the national park. The council marks a separate 2km dog route.
- **Maps** from the Manly Visitor Information Centre, right next to Manly Wharf.
- **Castle Rock** has a detour in place for upgrade works. Check the alerts before you go.

Start earlier than you think. The park closes at sunset and the last hour of this walk is the
prettiest.

## What is the last ferry home?

You finish at Manly Wharf, and the ferry is right there.

Monday to Thursday the last one to Circular Quay leaves at 12.20am. On a Friday or Saturday night it
is 12.50am. On a Sunday night it is 11.50pm.

The wharf gates close two minutes before the ferry is due. So the 12.20am is really a 12.18am, and
that is the version to remember at the end of a long day.

Check the [live times](/blog/live-sydney-train-bus-ferry-times/) before you start the last stretch. A
timetable is the plan. The countdown tells you what is actually running.

Walk it the other way and you finish on Spit Road in the dark, with your legs gone, waiting. Walk it
this way and you finish at a wharf with a ferry sitting on it.
