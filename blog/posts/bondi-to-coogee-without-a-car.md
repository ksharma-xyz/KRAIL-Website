Walk from one beach to the next, five times over, and swim at whichever one you like the look of.
On the way you pass a cemetery sitting right on the ocean edge, and between May and November,
whales.

Bring your swimmers and something to read. Five beaches in a row, and you will stop at more of them
than you meant to.

**It is a one way walk**, which is the whole reason to leave the car at home. Finish at Coogee and
you are at a different beach from the one you started at, a long way on foot from wherever you
parked. There is a bus, and there is one all night, and that is the rest of this page.

::: facts The walk, in short
Getting there: Route **333** from Circular Quay to Bondi Beach, about 40 minutes
Coming home: Route **373** from Coogee, all night, every night
Beaches: five, Bondi, Tamarama, Bronte, Clovelly and Coogee
Shade: almost none, it is a clifftop path the whole way
Prams: no, not the whole way
:::

## How do you get there without a car?

Catch the 333 from Circular Quay to Bondi Beach and get off at Campbell Parade. If you are not sure
which stop is yours, you can [find it by the route number](/blog/find-sydney-bus-stop-by-number/). It takes about 40
minutes: the 1.02pm from Circular Quay is at Bondi Beach at 1.42pm.

The walk starts at the southern end of Bondi, below the Icebergs pool. You will know it when you see
it, because everyone else is walking the same way.

## The walk itself

Waverley and Randwick councils manage half of this walk each, and neither of them publishes a
distance, a time or a grade that we could read. So take every number you have seen for this walk,
including the ones that used to be on this page, as somebody's estimate.

What nobody warns you about is the stairs. There are real climbs out of Tamarama and out of Bronte,
and they arrive after a flat opening stretch has convinced you this is a stroll. Nothing technical,
just more uphill than the reputation suggests.

There is **almost no shade the entire way**. It is a clifftop path, and by the middle of the day in
summer that is the defining feature of the walk rather than the view.

What you pass, in order: Bondi, Tamarama, Bronte, the Waverley Cemetery clifftop with headstones
sitting right on the ocean edge, Clovelly, then Coogee.

Between May and November there are whales, and the clifftop lookouts are as good a place to watch
them from as anywhere in Sydney.

## The practical stuff

- **Toilets** at every beach on the route: Bondi, Tamarama, Bronte, Clovelly, Coogee.
- **Water** fountains at Bondi, Tamarama, Bronte and Coogee.
- **Swimming**: Bondi Icebergs, the Bogey Hole at Bronte, Wylie's Baths at Coogee.
- **Coffee** at every beach. Bondi and Bronte have the most options.
- **Kids** manage it fine if they are confident walkers, but the Tamarama and Bronte stairs are real.
- **Prams**: no. Not the whole way.
- **Dogs**: read the signs at the beach you start from. Two different councils set the rules along
  this stretch, they change beach by beach, and we could not get either council to tell us what they
  are, so we are not printing hours you might get fined on.
- **After rain** the sandstone sections get slippery.
- Phone reception is good the whole way.

Early morning is quiet. Weekend afternoons and any summer day are not.

## Getting back, and the thing nobody tells you

The fear that puts people in a car is that you finish at the wrong beach with no way home. On this
walk it is not real, on any night of the week.

Route 373 leaves Coogee from Arden St opposite Coogee Bay Rd and runs all the way into the city, and
it does not stop for the night.

- **Sunday to Friday nights**, it goes at 12.11am, 12.31am, and then every half hour until 3.31am.
- **Saturday nights** are the thin ones, which is the opposite of what you would guess. After
  12.31am it drops to hourly: 1.31am, 2.31am, 3.31am.

At the Bondi end the 333 runs around the clock as well, every day, so if you change your mind
halfway and walk back you are still fine.

Either way, check the [live times](/blog/live-sydney-train-bus-ferry-times/) before you leave the
sand. A timetable is the plan. The countdown is what is actually happening.

That is the honest reason a transport app exists. Not to tell you the walk is nice, you already knew
that. To tell you that the thing you were worried about is not a problem.
