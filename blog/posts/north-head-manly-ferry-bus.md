Burragula lookout hands you the whole harbour at once, right across to the city skyline. Turn
around and it is open ocean to the horizon. The walk between those two views is one kilometre,
flat, with no steps anywhere on it.

Bring a hat and something cold to drink. You will stand at both lookouts for longer than you
planned to.

There is one catch, and it is the little bus up the hill. It finishes for the day far earlier than
you would ever guess. That part is on us.

::: facts The day, in short
Getting there: the **F1** ferry to Manly, then the **161** bus
The walk: **1km** loop, **30 minutes to an hour**, Grade 1
Underfoot: paved, two metres wide, **no steps**
Views: Burragula for the city, Yiningma for the ocean
Coming home: the last bus down goes at **5.24pm**
:::

## How do you get to North Head without a car?

Take the F1 from Circular Quay Wharf 3 to Manly. The 9.10am is at Manly Wharf at 9.32am.

At Manly, walk around to Stand C on East Esplanade. Take the 161 and get off at the stop called
Fairfax walk. The 9.42am is there at 9.53am.

Through the middle of the day the 161 leaves Manly at 12 and 42 minutes past the hour. The ride up
the hill takes about 12 minutes.

The 161 runs as a loop. The same bus carries on round the headland. It calls at your stop again on
the way down, so you go home from where you got off.

Want to see which stand is yours before you leave the house? You can
[find it on the map](/blog/find-sydney-stop-on-map/).

## What is the walk like?

The Fairfax walk is a 1km loop around the top of the headland. National Parks rates it Grade 1 and
allows 30 minutes to an hour.

It is paved the whole way and two metres wide. There are no steps on the loop at all. Prams and
wheelchairs get the same views as everyone else.

Burragula looks over the harbour to South Head and the city. A sandstone staircase goes up to it,
and so does a flat concrete path. Both lookouts have seats, so sit down and take your time.

Yiningma faces the other way, north along the cliffs and out to sea. The clifftops in between are
low heath. Nothing up here blocks the view.

The whales come through from June to July, then again from August to October. Turn up in summer and
the harbour is the show instead.

## What is up there?

- **Toilets** behind Bella Vista Cafe, opposite the North Head Sanctuary Visitor Centre.
- **A picnic shelter** sits between the two carparks, with no steps up to it.
- **Water, a hat and sunscreen** are the three things to bring.
- **Phone reception** is limited up on the headland.
- **Dogs** stay home, unless they are certified assistance animals.
- **Drones** are not allowed anywhere in the park.
- **Parking** costs $5 a vehicle a day, and you will stroll straight past it.

The park is open from sunrise to sunset. It can close for bad weather or fire danger.

## What time is the last bus down?

Here is the bit that catches people out.

The last 161 calling at the Fairfax walk stop leaves at 5.24pm. That is the same every day of the
week. It has you at Manly Wharf at 5.37pm.

The 161 does keep running after that. It just stops short of the top, turning around at the
Quarantine Station stop further down the hill. On a Saturday those go at 5.49pm, 6.49pm and 7.49pm.

So if you want the long summer evening up there, walk down North Head Scenic Drive afterwards and
pick the bus up lower down. Or keep walking and be in Manly for dinner.

The ferry is the easy half of the trip. On a Saturday night the last F1 leaves Manly at 12.50am and
is at Circular Quay at 1.12am. On a Sunday night it is 11.50pm, and on a weeknight 12.20am.
