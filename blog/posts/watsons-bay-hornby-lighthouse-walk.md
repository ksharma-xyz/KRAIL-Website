A red and white striped lighthouse, standing on the point where the harbour opens into the sea.
The whole walk out to it is one kilometre. Half an hour, maybe an hour if you stop, and you have
had the harbour on one side and the Pacific on the other.

The thing that puts people in a car is the feeling that South Head is miles from anywhere. It is a
23 minute boat ride. That part is on us.

Bring your swimmers. Camp Cove is right where the track starts.

::: facts The walk, in short
Getting there: the **F9** ferry from Circular Quay, 23 minutes to Watsons Bay
The walk: a **1km loop**, 30 minutes to an hour, grade 3
Starts at: **Camp Cove**, near the kiosk
Fare from Circular Quay: **$7.35** adult
Park hours: sunrise to sunset, every day
:::

## How do you get there without a car?

Take the F9 ferry from Circular Quay. It goes from Wharf 2.

On a Saturday the 10.15am is at Watsons Bay Wharf at 10.38am. Boats leave at quarter past and
quarter to all day.

Walk off the wharf and head for Camp Cove. It is about nine minutes on foot. The track starts near
the kiosk at the beach and it is signposted from there.

Buses 324, 325 and 380 also stop a short walk from Camp Cove, if the ferry does not suit. You can
[filter a trip to the mode you want](/blog/choose-sydney-transport-mode/) before you leave the
house.

## The walk itself

A one kilometre loop, graded 3. That means signposted, formed track, gentle hills and no experience
needed. It also means a lot of steps.

You set off from Camp Cove onto an 1870s cobblestone road. It takes you past Lady Bay Beach, which
is a nude bathing beach, so now you know.

Then the gun emplacements, which you can explore. Then the lighthouse at the end of the
point, painted in red and white stripes.

Stand up there and the harbour is behind you to the west. Middle Head and North Head sit across the
water to the north. Everything east of you is open ocean.

In whale season this is a lookout as much as a walk.

This stretch is part of the 80km Bondi to Manly Walk. Worth remembering on a day when one
kilometre is not enough.

## The practical stuff

- **Toilets** along the trail near Lady Bay Beach.
- **Steps**, many of them. Some of the ledges are rocky and uneven.
- **Kids** manage the track fine. Keep an eye on them near the edges.
- **Bring** a hat, sunscreen and water.
- **Phone reception** is limited inside the park. Sort out your trip home before you walk in.
- **Swimming** at the beaches here is unpatrolled and the rips can be strong.
- **Dogs** and drones stay at home. Assistance animals are welcome.
- **Daylight only**. The park is open sunrise to sunset.
- **Works** on the old military structures run through 2026 and 2027. The trail stays open.

## Getting back, and the thing nobody tells you

You do not have to leave when the park does. Watsons Bay keeps going long after the headland is
dark, and so do the boats.

Ferries leave Watsons Bay Wharf every half hour through the day. From 8.15pm they drop to hourly at
quarter past. The last one goes at 12.15am on a Saturday.

Miss it and the 324 bus leaves Military Rd opposite Robertson Park at 12.54am. That stop is about
four minutes from the wharf.

One more thing for right now. Rose Bay Wharf is closed for maintenance until 4 December. Until
then the F9 runs straight through to Watsons Bay with no stop on the way.
