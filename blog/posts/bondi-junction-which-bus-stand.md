Welcome to the quiet end of Bondi, my favourite end! There's a rock pool here that a lifesaver
and his team carved out of the sandstone by hand, and you get to swim in it. Then you get to
sit down to a seafood lunch.

The middle of the beach gets the crowds. Wander north and it feels like a whole other beach.

## The pool they carved by hand

Right at North Bondi there's a rock pool named after a man called Wally Weekes. It's easy to
stroll straight past, which is half the fun of finding it.

Here's the bit that surprised me. Wally led the team that carved the pool out of the foreshore
sandstone, so children would have a safe place to learn to swim. Nobody poured concrete. They
cut it out of the rock!

Wally was a lifesaver first. In 1907 he helped start the Bondi Surf and Social Lifesaving Club
and became its first captain. There's an archive photo from that year of the very first
clubhouse at North Bondi, and it was a tent. Someone even drew a big arrow on the photo to point
it out.

Pack a towel and your best paddling face, and check the water before you hop in.

## Lunch on Ramsgate Avenue

Next up is North Bondi Fish at 120 Ramsgate Avenue. You can have fresh oysters, fish and chips,
or pizza straight from the oven. The doors open at noon, and the kitchen keeps going until 10pm
Sunday to Thursday and 11pm on Friday and Saturday.

Rock pool first, oysters second. That's the order I'd pick every time.

## The whale that wasn't

Here's a story to tell your friends over lunch. Up on the cliffs above the beach, the Bondi
Golf Course has Aboriginal rock engravings, made by pecking outlines into the soft sandstone.
The biggest one is an eight-metre figure that people once took for a whale. Look closely at the
fins and it turns out to be a shark!

This one is a story to enjoy rather than a stop on your day. The engravings matter a lot to
Aboriginal people today. Look with your eyes only, and they'll stay just as they are for the next
visitor.

## A swim with a story

Fancy swimming proper laps instead of paddling? The Bondi Icebergs pool sits at the south end of
the beach, and the club behind it started in 1929. Have a quick look at the club's site for
opening times before you go, because they change.

## Getting to North Bondi from Bondi Junction

From Bondi Junction, head to Stand D and hop on the 379. It runs out to North Bondi, and the 1pm
weekday bus pulls in at North Bondi Shops at 1.23pm, right by the pool and lunch.

Stand D is on the Oxford Street side of the station, and there are stairs, escalators and a lift
down to the buses.

Want the main beach instead? The 333 from Stand A gets you to Bondi Beach on Campbell Parade at
1.14pm for the 1pm weekday bus, and there's one about every six minutes. From there you can
stroll north along the sand.

Heading home, there's a lift straight up to the trains. Either platform, 1 or 2, takes you back
towards the city.

## Out late? Getting home is easy

Stayed out till the stars came out? No stress, trains run late. The last train from Bondi
Junction leaves at 12.55am Monday to Thursday, 1.35am on Friday, 1.38am on Saturday and 12.48am
on Sunday. Those are the times for an ordinary week in October 2026, so peek at the
[live times](/blog/live-sydney-train-bus-ferry-times/) before a big night.

Want the whole coast while you're here? The
[walk from Bondi to Coogee](/blog/bondi-to-coogee-without-a-car/) links up all the beaches.

Now go and find the pool that was cut from the rock!
