Ninety-one metres up, at the most northern point of Sydney, stands a lighthouse built out of the hill
it sits on. Broken Bay one way. Open ocean the other.

It is about a kilometre up from the car park, and around thirty minutes. Getting to Palm Beach is the
part everyone assumes needs a car. That part is on us.

::: facts The day, in short
Getting there: Route **199**, Manly Wharf to Palm Beach by way of Mona Vale
The walk up: about **1km**, around 30 minutes each way
Going inside: **Sunday tours only**, 11am to 3pm, booked ahead
Coming home: the 199 runs **all night**, every night
Dogs: no, it is a national park
:::

::: callout Do this first
Book a tour if you want to go inside the lighthouse. The outside is yours any day you walk up there,
no booking and no fuss.
:::

## How do you get there without a car?

Take the 199. It runs the whole length of the peninsula, from Manly Wharf up to Palm Beach.

On a Sunday it leaves Manly Wharf at 9.50am. It passes Mona Vale at 10.32am and is at Palm Beach at
11.08am.

From the city it is quicker to come in two steps. Take the B1 from Wynyard to Mona Vale, then change
to the 199 there. The B1 runs seven days a week, 4.30am to 12.30am, so there is nothing to plan.

Get off at Palm Beach Wharf. If you want to find that stop ahead of time, you can
[look it up by the route number](/blog/find-sydney-bus-stop-by-number/).

Rather start closer? Stay on a little longer and get off at Palm Beach Golf Club, on Beach Rd. That
is the stop nearest the car park where the track begins.

## What is the walk up like?

The Barrenjoey track leaves from the Governor Phillip parking area. It is about a kilometre and takes
around thirty minutes each way.

It is an easy and picturesque climb, and it still asks for moderate fitness. There is a beach
crossing at the start. Then about ten steps. Then some very steep sections with small step-ups
between the sandstone.

Want the climb over with sooner? Smugglers track is the steeper and shorter way to the top.

The lighthouse went up in 1881. The sandstone was quarried on the spot and nobody ever painted it.

From up there you look over Broken Bay, Ku-ring-gai Chase National Park and the Central Coast.
Between May and September there are whales out front. Bring binoculars.

## Can you go inside the lighthouse?

Only on a guided tour. Tours run most Sundays, on the hour from 11am to 3pm. Each one takes half an
hour.

It is $15 for an adult, $13 concession and $11 for a child. Booking is required, so sort it before
you leave home.

Allow at least forty minutes to get up to the meeting point. The walk is 1.1km with steep bits, and
you do not want to be racing it.

## What else is handy to know?

- **Toilets** at the parking area and again at the lighthouse. The one up top is accessible and has a
  baby change table.
- **Drinking water** at the parking area, and a bottle refill station at the lighthouse.
- **Dogs**: no. It is a national park, and only assistance animals are allowed.
- **Swimming**: the beaches here are not patrolled and can carry strong rips.
- **Phone reception** is limited up there, so load your map before you set off.
- **Access** is rated hard. A beach crossing, steps and very steep sections.
- **Parking** at the Governor Phillip carpark is paid, and you cannot drive up to the lighthouse
  anyway.
- **Weather**: the park can close for strong wind, heavy rain or fire danger.

Have a look at the local alerts the morning you go. Then you will know what is open before you are
on the bus.

## When is the last bus home?

Here is the thing that puts people in a car. You picture yourself at Palm Beach after dark with the
whole peninsula between you and your bed. On this route that worry is not real.

The 199 runs right through the night, every night of the week.

On a Sunday the last evening bus leaves Palm Beach at 11.36pm and reaches Mona Vale at 11.56pm. From
there it goes hourly: 12.36am, 1.36am, 2.36am and 3.36am.

Saturday nights run thicker. After the 11.35pm it is every half hour, 12.06am and 12.36am and on
through to 3.36am.

If you want the B1 on to the city at the end, be at Mona Vale before 12.30am. Any later and the 199
keeps rolling, all the way down to Manly Wharf.

Check the [live times](/blog/live-sydney-train-bus-ferry-times/) before you start back down the hill.
A timetable is the plan. The countdown is what is actually running.

Then get on the bus with sand in your shoes and let somebody else do the driving.
