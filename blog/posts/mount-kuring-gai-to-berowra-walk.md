Walk off the end of a suburban street and straight into the bush. Ten kilometres later you climb
out at a different station.

At the bottom of it all is Cowan Creek. Waratah Bay on the way holds the rusting frame of an old
boatshed, and that frame is all that is left. Apple Tree Bay sits a short way off the track with a
picnic area and a reason to sit down.

Pack lunch and give the whole day to it.

**You finish at a different station from the one you started at.** That is the reason to leave the
car at home. Park at Mount Kuring-gai and your car spends the evening there while you stand at
Berowra. A train runs every half hour instead, and that part is on us.

::: facts The walk, in short
Distance: **10km** one way, 3 hours 30 minutes to 4 hours 30 minutes
Grade: **3**, many steps and short steep hills
Getting there: a train to **Mount Kuring-gai**, every 30 minutes on a weekend
Coming home: a train from **Berowra**, about every half hour through the afternoon
Toilets: at the two stations, nowhere in between
Dogs: not allowed in the national park
:::

## How do you get there without a car?

1. Take a train to Mount Kuring-gai. On a weekend the 8.45am from Central is there at 9.46am.
2. Walk east from the station to the end of Harwood Avenue. The track starts there.
3. Follow it down through the woodland towards Cowan Creek.
4. At the creek the track branches. Turn south for the short walk to Apple Tree Bay, or north along
   the water for Waratah Bay.
5. From Waratah Bay, climb out to Berowra station.

The Berowra end sits east of the station, across the freeway. Come up that way and you are a few
minutes from the platform.

## The walk itself

It is a grade 3 track, which means many steps and short steep hills. The track is formed but it has
obstacles in places. Some bushwalking experience is recommended.

The climb to Berowra is the steep part, and it waits for you at the end of the day.

So give it the full four and a half hours. Start in the morning and plan nothing for the evening.

Along the way the track passes dense woodland and an Aboriginal midden. Bring binoculars if birds
are your thing.

Summer out here averages 17 to 31 degrees. There is no water on the track, so carry all of it.

## The practical stuff

- **Toilets** at Mount Kuring-gai station and at Berowra station. Nothing in between.
- **Water**: all of it goes in your pack. There is nowhere to fill up.
- **Lunch**: Apple Tree Bay picnic area, down a side track from the creek.
- **Steps**: many of them, so prams will not get down.
- **Dogs** are not allowed in the national park. Certified assistance animals are the exception.
- **Navigation**: carry a topographic map and compass, or a GPS.
- **Phone reception** is limited. You need it to call Triple Zero, so tell someone your plan first.
- **Closures** happen in poor weather and on high fire danger days. Check the morning you go.
- **Parking** is available at both stations, if someone is dropping you off.

Fill in a trip intention form before you start. Then someone knows where you are.

## What time is the last train home?

The worry with a walk like this is finishing in the dark at a station you have never used. Berowra
is the end of the T1, and the trains out of it keep going late.

On a Saturday, Sunday or public holiday, trains leave Berowra for the city about every half hour
through the afternoon. The last one goes at 12.06am and reaches Central at 1.01am.

Before it, there is one at 10.38pm. Nothing runs in the hour and a half between those two.

Monday to Friday there is more room at the end of the night. Trains go at 11.43pm, 12.13am and
12.43am. Then 1.13am and 1.43am.

Pick your train before you start the climb out of the valley. Check the
[live times](/blog/live-sydney-train-bus-ferry-times/) once your phone has reception again. A
timetable is the plan. The countdown is the train.

If you finish this one with legs to spare, there is another walk between two stations nearby. It
runs from [Berowra to Cowan](/blog/berowra-to-cowan-without-a-car/), and it is a harder day.
