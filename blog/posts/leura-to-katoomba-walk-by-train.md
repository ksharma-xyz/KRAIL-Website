Welcome to the Blue Mountains! Today you walk a cliff edge with a giant valley beside you the whole
way.

Honestly? One of the best days out you can have from Sydney.

You start near Leura and finish in Katoomba, and the train brings you home. It's a one-way walk,
which is the best kind, because you never walk back the way you came.

Pack a lunch and keep your camera handy. There are lookouts the whole way along, and you'll stop at
way more of them than you ever planned to, which is exactly the point. Worth it.

::: facts The walk, in short
Getting there: take the train to **Leura**, then stroll **1.8km** to Gordon Falls
The walk: **8km** one way, **3hrs to 4hrs**, Grade 3
Underfoot: short steep hills and **many steps**
Finish: Scenic World, then an easy **2.5km** down to Katoomba station
Coming home: the last train out of Katoomba is **11.08pm** on a weekend
:::

## How do you get to Leura?

Jump on the 8.24am from Central, the intercity towards Lithgow. Put your feet up, watch the suburbs
turn into bush, and you'll step off at Leura at 10.22am. Slept in? No stress, there's one at 24
minutes past every hour on a weekend.

From Leura station it's a 1.8km stroll to Gordon Falls picnic area, and that's your starting line.
The track heads off from Gordon Falls lookout towards Katoomba. Off you go!

Going on a weekday? Catch the 9.09am from Central and you'll be at Leura by 11.05am, with heaps of
day left for the cliffs.

Want to see the stop before you leave the house? You can
[find it on the map](/blog/find-sydney-stop-on-map/).

## What's the walk like?

It's 8km one way, and National Parks reckons 3 to 4 hours. They rate it Grade 3, which means a
proper walk with a bit of bounce in it.

The track is formed and clearly signposted, with short steep hills and a lot of steps. If you've got
a moderate level of fitness and a bit of bushwalking behind you, you'll have a ball.

The whole way you're on the cliff edge above the Jamison Valley, and Mount Solitary sits across the
gap for most of it. Picture a giant valley at your elbow and the Three Sisters waiting up ahead.

Leura Cascades turns up early. Then you cross Buttenshaw Bridge, which has been standing there since
1935.

Then, the big one. Echo Point and the Three Sisters, a declared Aboriginal Place standing in the
land of the Gundungurra and Darug People. The council's visitor centre at the lookout is open every day from 9am
to 4pm, so pop in and say hi.

Keep your ears open on the quiet stretches. Lyrebirds live out here, and so do yellow-tailed black
cockatoos and crimson rosellas. Spot all three and you've had a very big day.

## What's at each end?

Gordon Falls picnic area kicks things off. It looks right out over the valley, and there's a
playground if you've brought the kids.

Katoomba is the finish line. The track comes out at Scenic World, and the cafes are in the village
just beyond. Time to sit down and celebrate with something delicious.

## What to pack and where to stop

- **Toilets** are waiting at Gordon Falls, Echo Point, Katoomba Falls Reserve and Scenic World.
- **Drinking water** and picnic tables are along the way, and it's nice to start with a full bottle.
- **Food** is easy at both ends, in Leura village and Katoomba village. Bring lunch for the middle.
- **Pack** a hat, sunscreen and a raincoat, plus sturdy shoes and a first aid kit.
- **Kids** will love the playground at the Gordon Falls end, and older ones who like a big walk will love the rest.
- **Prams and wheelchairs** can get to Echo Point lookout easily, and the view there is a beauty.
- **Your camera** gets the shots today, and the cockatoos get the sky all to themselves, because it's a drone-free park.
- **Your phone** gets to be gloriously offline for stretches of the track, so grab the map at Leura first.

## How do you get home?

This is the lovely part. The trains run late. Really late.

From Scenic World it's an easy 2.5km stroll down to Katoomba station. A bus runs to Scenic World too,
so have a look at what's leaving if your legs have had enough.

On a weekend the trains leave Katoomba at 38 minutes past every hour, so the 4.38pm has you back at
Central by 6.41pm. You'll be home in time for dinner!

Want to stay for sunset and a long dinner in the village? The last train leaves Katoomba at 11.08pm
and gets you to Central at 1.12am. On a weekday it's 10.16pm out of Katoomba and into Central at
12.17am.

Legs done halfway? You can jump on at Leura instead, because the same train stops there three
minutes after Katoomba. There's one an hour either way, so if you just miss one, that's your excuse
for another coffee.

Heads up, on 28 and 29 November buses are doing the train's job between the mountains and
Blacktown. Give yourself a bit of extra time that day and enjoy the bus ride.
