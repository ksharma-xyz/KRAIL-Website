The clifftop track from Leura to Katoomba is 8km, and it runs one way. There is a station at each
end. Get off the train at Leura in the morning, walk the cliff edge, and catch the train home from
Katoomba.

Drive instead and the car spends the day 8km behind you. That is the whole case for the train.

Pack lunch. There are lookouts the whole way, and you will stop at more of them than you planned to.

::: facts The walk, in short
Getting there: the train to **Leura**, then **1.8km** on foot to Gordon Falls
The walk: **8km** one way, **3hrs to 4hrs**, Grade 3
Underfoot: short steep hills and **many steps**
At the far end: **2.5km** from Scenic World to Katoomba station
Coming home: the last train out of Katoomba is **11.08pm** on a weekend
:::

## How do you get there without a car?

1. Catch an intercity train from Central towards Lithgow. On a weekend they leave at 24 minutes past
   the hour.
2. The 8.24am from Central is at Leura at 10.22am.
3. Walk from Leura station to Gordon Falls picnic area. It is 1.8km.
4. Start at Gordon Falls lookout and follow the track towards Katoomba.

Monday to Friday they leave Central at nine minutes past the hour through the middle of the day. The
9.09am is at Leura at 11.05am.

If you want the stop on a map before you leave the house, you can
[find it on the map](/blog/find-sydney-stop-on-map/).

## The walk itself

It is 8km one way. National Parks suggests 3 to 4 hours and calls it Grade 3.

The track is formed and clearly signposted. The hills are short and steep, and there are many steps.
Some bushwalking experience is recommended, and the day asks for a moderate level of fitness.

You walk the cliff edge above the Jamison Valley. Mount Solitary sits across it for most of the way.

Leura Cascades comes early. Buttenshaw Bridge, built in 1935, is on the route too.

Then Echo Point, and the Three Sisters. It is a declared Aboriginal Place, in the land of the
Gundungurra and Darug People. The council runs a visitor centre at the lookout, open daily from 9am
to 4pm.

Listen for lyrebirds on the quiet stretches. Yellow-tailed black cockatoos and crimson rosellas are
up here as well.

Phone reception is limited along the track. Download what you need before you leave Leura.

## What is at each end?

Gordon Falls picnic area starts the day. It looks out over the valley, and there is a playground for
the kids.

Katoomba ends it. The track comes out at Scenic World, and the village beyond it has the cafes.

## The practical stuff

- **Toilets** at Gordon Falls, Echo Point, Katoomba Falls Reserve and Scenic World.
- **Drinking water** and picnic tables along the walk. Fill your bottle before you start.
- **Food** in Leura village at one end and Katoomba village at the other. Carry lunch for the middle.
- **Bring** a hat, sunscreen and a raincoat. Sturdy shoes and a first aid kit too.
- **Kids** get a playground at the Gordon Falls end, but the walk is long and full of steps.
- **Wheelchairs and prams**: not on this track. Echo Point lookout is the part that is easy to reach.
- **Dogs** are not allowed in the national park.
- **Drones** are not allowed either.
- **Phone reception** is limited, so load the map before you start.

## How do you get home?

The worry with a one way walk is finishing somewhere with no way out. This one finishes at a station,
and the trains run late.

From Scenic World it is 2.5km to Katoomba station. A bus also runs to Scenic World, so look at what
is leaving if your legs are done.

On a weekend the trains go from Katoomba at 38 minutes past the hour. The 4.38pm is at Central at
6.41pm.

The last one leaves at 11.08pm and gets to Central at 1.12am. Monday to Friday it is earlier, 10.16pm
out of Katoomba and into Central at 12.17am.

Turn back early and Leura works the same way, three minutes before Katoomba on every trip towards the
city. Either way it is one train an hour, so missing one costs an hour.

One weekend to avoid. On Saturday 28 and Sunday 29 November, buses replace the trains between
Blacktown and the mountains. Pick another day.
