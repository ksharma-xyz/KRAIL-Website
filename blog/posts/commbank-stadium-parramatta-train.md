CommBank Stadium has no public parking on game days. Take the train to Parramatta, then walk 15
minutes via Church Street and Victoria Road. Your match ticket pays for the trip in both directions,
right through until 4am.

The worry with a night match is the trip home. Three streets around the ground close once the crowd
starts leaving. On one of the three match nights the light rail stops running as well. Both of those
are easier to deal with if you know about them now.

::: facts The match days, in short
When: **Friday 16 and Friday 23 October**, and **Sunday 1 November**
Station: **Parramatta**, then a 15 minute walk, or the light rail one leg
Your fare: **included in the match ticket**, both ways, until 4am
Light rail: **5am to 1am** daily, but not past Parramatta Square on 23 October
Parking: **none** at the stadium on game days
:::

Keep the ticket in your pocket once you are through the gates. It is your fare on the train, the
metro and the bus. The light rail and the ferry too. Show it to staff when you board. Keep it safe
for the trip back, because without it you pay your own way home.

## How do you get there?

Take the train to Parramatta. It is the closest station to the ground, and from there you have two
ways in.

Walk it. Head out via Church Street and Victoria Road, which takes about 15 minutes.

Or take the light rail one leg. Board at Parramatta Square and get off at Prince Alfred Square, then
continue on foot along Victoria Road.

Coming by bus instead? Go to Stand A4 at Parramatta Interchange. Six routes stop on O'Connell
Street, directly outside the stadium. They are the 600, 601, 603, 604, 606 and 706. Route 52 stops
on Victoria Road, a short walk away. All of them are accessible.

Coming by ferry? Parramatta Wharf is the closest one. Walk to the Robin Thomas light rail stop along
the riverside and Argus Lane, then ride to Prince Alfred Square.

## Which day are you going?

- **Friday 16 October.** Gates open 4.45pm. The men's Samoa v France kicks off at 5.45pm, the women's Australia v Samoa at 8.05pm.
- **Friday 23 October.** Gates open 5.15pm. The women's Samoa v Wales kicks off at 5.45pm, the men's Tonga v Lebanon at 8.05pm.
- **Sunday 1 November.** Gates open 3pm, and there are three matches. The women's Australia v Wales starts at 3.40pm. Then the men's Lebanon v France at 5.40pm, and the men's Tonga v Samoa at 8.05pm.

## Friday 23 October is the awkward one

Three things land on that one night, and none of them is about the football.

Parramatta Lanes is on in the city centre, and thousands of people go to it. Roads close for it. The
light rail will not run between Parramatta Square and Westmead from 5pm to 11.30pm, with buses
replacing it.

So on the 23rd, plan to walk from Parramatta station. Fifteen minutes on foot beats working out
which replacement bus goes where.

There is one more thing for anyone heading into the city afterwards. From 10.40pm that night there
are no trains between Wynyard and Central, and none around the city loop. The 8.05pm match finishes
close to that. Use the metro, the light rail or a local bus to get across town, and check your route
before you leave the ground.

The clash has an upside. The festival is in the city centre, which is the way you walk in from the
station. Go early and make an evening of it.

## What about getting home?

The light rail runs until 1am daily, so even the 8.05pm match leaves you time. On 23 October it
starts again at 11.30pm.

Roads around the ground close as the crowd leaves, at the direction of police. Three of them:

- **O'Connell Street**, from Grose Street to George Street.
- **Ross Street**, from O'Connell Street to Trott Street.
- **Victoria Road**, from O'Connell Street to Villiers Street.

That moves the bus stops outside the stadium. Two of them are worth knowing before you walk out.

- **Back towards Parramatta station**, use O'Connell Street at Fennell Street.
- **Towards the Hills, Epping or Macquarie Park**, use O'Connell Street opposite Harold Street.

Staff will be out there, and the signs change too.

## If you were going to drive

There is no public parking at the stadium itself on game days. Four car parks sit within walking
distance, and all of them will be in demand.

- **Eat Street**, entry off George Street. Closed on 23 October for the festival.
- **Justice Precinct**, entry off Hunter Street.
- **Parramatta Station car park**, entry off Wentworth Street.
- **Parramatta Leagues Club**, on O'Connell Street.

The version that works is driving to a Park & Ride and catching the train in. Park & Ride gives you
up to 18 hours of free parking a day. You just have to complete a public transport trip, tapping on
and tapping off. Your match ticket covers the parking as well. On the way out, press the intercom at
the exit gate and show the ticket to the operator. You can
[check the spaces before you leave](/blog/sydney-park-and-ride-live-spaces/).

## The practical stuff

- **Taxi rank** on the eastern side of Villiers Street, between Ross Street and Victoria Road.
- **No drop-offs on O'Connell Street.** It is a no-stopping zone at all times, for every vehicle.
- **Rideshare** pick-ups are worth arranging away from the ground, out of the traffic.
- **Gates** open between 30 minutes and an hour before the first kick off, depending on the day.
- **Accessible buses** leave from Stand A4 and stop right outside, and the light rail is accessible too.
- **Driving close** puts you in the queue everybody else is in. Church Street and Victoria Road both get heavier, and so does James Ruse Drive.

published timetable is the plan. The countdown is what is actually turning up.
