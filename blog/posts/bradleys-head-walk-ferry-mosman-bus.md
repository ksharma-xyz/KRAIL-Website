Catch the ferry to Taronga Zoo wharf, then walk away from the zoo. The track runs 4km around the
headlands to Chowder Bay. The last bus out of Chowder Bay goes at 7.38pm.

There is no parking at the wharf where the walk starts. Park at the far end instead and your car
sits 4km away from you all afternoon. That is the whole case for the ferry.

Bring your swimmers. There is a beach waiting at the other end.

::: facts The trip, in short
Getting there: the **F2** ferry from Circular Quay to **Taronga Zoo**, about 12 minutes
The walk: **4km** one way, **1hr 30min to 2hr 30min**, Grade 3
Underfoot: short steep hills and **many steps**
At the far end: a beach, toilets and three places to eat
Coming home: the last **111** bus leaves Chowder Bay at **7.38pm**
:::

## How do you get there without a car?

1. Catch the F2 ferry from Circular Quay towards Taronga Zoo. Check the wharf number on the board
   before you board.
2. On a weekend they go every 20 minutes. The 9.52am from Circular Quay is at Taronga Zoo at
   10.04am.
3. Monday to Friday it is every half hour through the middle of the day. The 9.50am is in at
   10.02am.
4. Get off and turn away from the zoo entrance. The track starts at the wharf.
5. Follow the signs to Bradleys Head, then on to Chowder Bay.

If you are not sure which wharf is yours, [find it on the map](/blog/find-sydney-stop-on-map/)
before you leave the house.

## The walk itself

It is 4km one way. National Parks suggests 1hr 30min to 2hr 30min and calls it Grade 3, with some
bushwalking experience recommended.

What the grade does not tell you is the steps. There are many of them. The hills between them are
short and steep, and the track is formed but has some obstacles.

Bradleys Head is the popular lookout along the way. There are military relics there and an
amphitheatre looking out at Fort Denison. The Opera House and the Harbour Bridge sit across the water.

The end of Bradleys Head Wharf is closed. Inspections found the structure had deteriorated, so
access is restricted.

Athol Hall is on the route and you can stop there for a drink.

Fox baiting runs across these headlands until 31 January. Do not touch a bait or an ejector device.

Mobile reception is limited along the track. Load what you need before you get on the ferry.

## What is at the Chowder Bay end?

Clifton Gardens Reserve has the beach, the lawns and a shaded playground. There is sheltered seating
and there are barbecues.

Three places to eat sit in the heritage buildings above the water. Seals turn up here regularly, and
penguins and whales come through with the seasons.

## The practical stuff

- **Toilets** near the cafe at Chowder Bay and at Clifton Gardens Reserve, including an accessible
  one.
- **Swimming** at the beach in Clifton Gardens Reserve, with snorkelling and kayaking along the
  foreshore.
- **Lifeguards**: none. The beaches in this park are not patrolled and the rips can be strong.
- **Food** at three places at Chowder Bay, and a drink at Athol Hall on the way.
- **Picnic** on the lawns at Clifton Gardens Reserve, or at the Bradleys Head picnic area.
- **Water and a hat**: carry both. National Parks asks for it on this walk.
- **Steps**: many of them, with short steep hills in between.
- **Wheelchairs and prams**: not on this track. The surface is unpaved.
- **Dogs** are not allowed in the park at all.
- **Phone reception** is limited, so download the map before you start.
- **Closing time**: the park is open sunrise to sunset, and so is the track gate at Chowder Bay.

## How do you get home?

The worry with a one way walk is finishing somewhere with no way out. Chowder Bay has one bus, so
the time matters more here than it does on most walks.

The 111 goes from Chowder Bay Road, at the stop opposite the marine science institute. It leaves at
38 minutes past the hour for most of the day.

The last one is 7.38pm. That is the same on a Saturday and a Sunday. It is at South Mosman Wharf at
7.52pm, and the ferry from there leaves at 8.26pm and reaches Circular Quay at 8.40pm.

If you miss the one you wanted, the next 111 does not come into Chowder Bay at all. It starts back
out on Middle Head Road about half an hour later.

The 111 also runs out to Military Road at Mosman. The 100 goes through from there to the city at the
QVB, if a bus suits you better than a ferry.

Rather walk back to the ferry you came in on? Start early. The last F2 from Taronga Zoo goes
at 6.54pm on a weekend and 7.01pm Monday to Friday.
